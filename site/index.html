kyfram is a keyframe-driven rendering engine. You write down what shapes look like at specific times. Go owns the tweening and renders it to video.

That ball is two keyframes and an easing curve. The script is twelve lines. Scroll down.

Install

curl -fsSL https://codetesla51.gitlab.io/kyfram/install.sh | sh

Needs Go 1.25+ and ffmpeg. No prebuilt binaries to track. The script clones and builds for your machine. Specific version: sh -s -- v0.2.0.

Quickstart

examples/intro.lgv 
scene(2, 30)

shape("ball", "circle", table{
    "0": table{x: 100, y: 180, r: 40, color: "#e64c40"},
    "2": table{x: 540, y: 180, r: 40, color: "#e64c40"},
})
kyfram render examples/intro.lgv --out out.mp4

Quality flags ride along: --crf 18 --preset slow for near-lossless, --format png for a single frame with no ffmpeg.

kyfram view examples/intro.lgv

Opens localhost:8080 with a scrub slider and hot reload. Save the file, the preview follows.

What it does

Declarative keyframes

States at times, quoted in seconds. Numbers and colors lerp, text snaps. Retiming is editing a number.

Easing per segment

Linear, ease in and out, cubic, sine. Declared on the earlier key, owned by the engine.

Rich primitives

Circle, rect, polygon, star, wave, path, text on a path, images. Gradients and blend modes included.

Groups and camera

Group children around a local origin. Pan and zoom the whole scene with keyframed cameras.

Audio and voice

Mux tracks under the video. Neural text to speech returns word timings for synced captions.

Built for batch

Template vars and text files turn one script into many videos. Preview hot, render cold.
